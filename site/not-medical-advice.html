Not medical advice

This is information, not medical advice. It cannot tell you whether you can join a study. Ask your doctor or the study team.

What this site gives you

Trial Finder helps you find clinical studies and read what they say about themselves. It helps you prepare for a conversation with your doctor or a study team. It does not take the place of that conversation.

Who decides whether you can take part

Only the study team can, after their own checks. Many of a study's rules need tests, records or a clinician's judgment. The notes the AI writes beside a study's rules are a starting point for questions. They may be wrong, and they are not a decision. The study's own words, shown on the page, are what count.

Your treatment

Please do not start, stop or change any treatment or medicine because of anything on this site. Talk with your doctor first.

Taking part is your choice

Joining a study is voluntary. Before you decide, it may help to ask the study team what taking part involves, its possible risks and benefits, what it costs you, and how to leave if you change your mind. The sheet you can print from this site lists questions like these.

About the records

The people who run each study write its record and are responsible for it. The U.S. government does not review or approve every study that is listed. ClinicalTrials.gov explains this in its own disclaimer.

To learn more about how studies work, ClinicalTrials.gov has a plain guide: Learn about studies.

In an emergency

If you or someone near you needs urgent help, call your local emergency number now. This site cannot help in an emergency.

Trial Finder is not affiliated with or endorsed by the U.S. National Institutes of Health, the National Library of Medicine or ClinicalTrials.gov. A study being listed there does not mean the U.S. government has reviewed or approved it.
